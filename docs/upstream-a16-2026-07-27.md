# Qualcomm posted a device tree for OUR laptop — research, 2026-07-27

Research session while the box was offline. Headline: **on 2026-07-21, six days ago,
Konrad Dybcio (Qualcomm) posted an upstream device tree for the ASUS Zenbook A16
UX3607OA — the exact machine — and it reports Display and Audio working.** Reviewed by
Dmitry Baryshkov and Abel Vesa. It is not yet merged (`New / Action Required` in
patchwork as of today).

## ★★★ Delta 1 — our eDP PHY supplies may point at the WRONG PMIC

Upstream:

```dts
&mdss_dp3_phy {
	vdda-phy-supply = <&vreg_l2f_e1>;   /* ldo2 of PMIC F_E1, 880 mV */
	vdda-pll-supply = <&vreg_l4f_e1>;   /* ldo4 of PMIC F_E1, 1.2 V  */
};
```

and the `&apps_rsc` bank order in that DTS is:

| node | compatible | `qcom,pmic-id` |
|---|---|---|
| regulators-0 | pmh0101 | B_E0 |
| regulators-1 | pmcx0102 | C_E0 |
| regulators-2 | pmh0110 | F_E0 |
| **regulators-3** | pmcx0102 | **C_E1** |
| **regulators-4** | pmh0110 | **F_E1** ← the eDP PHY's rails live here |

Our live DT resolves `vdda-phy` → `rsc@18900000/**regulators-3**/ldo2` and `vdda-pll` →
`…/regulators-3/ldo4` (decoded from `/proc/device-tree` on 2026-07-25). If our bank order
matches upstream's, **we have been pointing the eDP PHY at ldo2/ldo4 of C_E1 instead of
F_E1 — a different PMIC.**

Corroborating evidence from our own notes, which reads very differently in this light:
we recorded "vdda-phy = ldo2, **this PHY is its only consumer in the whole tree**" and
"vdda-pll = ldo4, shared only with two PCIe PHYs that never probe, so it likely has no
other vote and really powers off." Upstream's `vreg_l2f_e1`/`vreg_l4f_e1` are shared —
they also feed `vdda-qrefrpt{0,1,2}-0p9`, `vdda-qrefrx{0,1,2}-0p9` and
`vdda-refgen4-1p2` on the USB/PCIe PHYs. **On a correct tree those rails have other
voters and `regulator_bulk_disable()` from the eDP PHY changes nothing physically. On
ours they have no other voter, so they genuinely switch off** — and if they are the wrong
LDOs, we are cutting power to something else entirely at exactly the moment the box dies,
with the ADSP the first casualty.

🔍 **NOT YET VERIFIED — first thing to run when the box is up:**

```bash
for d in /proc/device-tree/soc@0/rsc@18900000/regulators-*; do
    echo -n "$d: "; tr -d '\0' < $d/qcom,pmic-id; echo
done
```

If `regulators-3` reports `C_E1`, the mis-wiring is confirmed and it is a one-line DT fix.

## ★★ Delta 2 — wrong panel driver, and the power-down sequencing is the difference

| | ours (live DT) | upstream |
|---|---|---|
| panel `compatible` | `"edp-panel"` (generic `panel-edp`) | **`"samsung,atna33xc20"`** |
| `enable-gpios` | present | `<&tlmm 18 GPIO_ACTIVE_HIGH>` |
| panel `power-supply` | present | `<&vreg_edp>` = fixed 3.3 V reg on **`<&tlmm 70>`** |
| backlight enable | — | `pinctrl-0 = <&edp_bl_en>` |
| `mdss_dp3` pinctrl | **none** | `pinctrl-0 = <&edp0_hpd_default>`, function `edp0_hot` |

Upstream's binding for this panel exists precisely because **its power sequencing is
non-standard** — it is an AMOLED eDP panel and cannot be driven by the generic path. From
`panel-samsung-atna33xc20.c` (saved in `upstream/`), the disable path is:

- `el_on3` GPIO (our `enable-gpios`) → 0, then **`msleep(20)`**;
- the 3.3 V supply is dropped via **`pm_runtime` with a 2000 ms autosuspend delay**, i.e.
  *not* immediately at unprepare;
- a **minimum off-time is enforced** before the next enable (`msleep()` computed from the
  last-off timestamp);
- HPD is used rather than assumed.

The generic `panel-edp` path drops the enable GPIO and the rail back-to-back with no such
sequencing. Note this also explains the TLMM 18 / 70 pins our own notes flagged as
load-bearing ("do not re-free TLMM 18/70") without knowing why: **18 is the panel EL_ON3
enable, 70 is the eDP 3.3 V rail enable.**

## Delta 3 — the eDP HPD pin is never muxed on our tree

Upstream muxes `edp0_hpd_default` (`function = "edp0_hot"`) on `&mdss_dp3`. We set no
pinctrl there at all. The A14 does the same thing upstream. Unknown whether this matters
for the crash; it is free to adopt.

## What this reframes

- **The crash is very likely a device-tree defect on our side, not a silicon or driver
  bug.** Two independent errors sit exactly in the code path that kills the box: the
  supplies `qcom_edp_phy_exit()` disables, and the panel unprepare that precedes it.
- It explains cleanly why nobody upstream hits this: on x1e80100 and on Konrad's A16 tree,
  the same `qcom_edp_phy_exit()` runs and is harmless.
- It does **not** yet explain `PHYSKIP=2` (skip `regulator_bulk_disable`) still dying.
  Either the clock half is independently wrong too, or the panel sequencing is a second
  sufficient cause. Adopting the whole DTS tests both at once.

# ⛔ CORRECTION (same day, 13:5x) — DELTAS 1 AND 2 ABOVE ARE **WRONG**. Verified against our own DTS.

Both were inferred from a `/proc/device-tree` directory listing without reading the actual
property values. Our flat `dts/test69.dts` resolves them definitively.

## Delta 1 (wrong PMIC) — **FALSE**

Phandles resolved out of the decompiled tree:

```
vdda-phy-supply -> 0xb0 -> soc@0/rsc@18900000/regulators-3/ldo2
vdda-pll-supply -> 0xad -> soc@0/rsc@18900000/regulators-3/ldo4
```

and our bank order is **not** Konrad's:

| node | ours | Konrad's |
|---|---|---|
| regulators-0 | B_E0 | B_E0 |
| regulators-1 | **C_E1** | C_E0 |
| regulators-2 | F_E0 | F_E0 |
| regulators-3 | **F_E1** | C_E1 |
| regulators-4 | **H_E0** | F_E1 |

Our `regulators-3` carries `qcom,pmic-id = "F_E1"` — **the same PMIC upstream uses.** So
our `vdda-phy`/`vdda-pll` are ldo2/ldo4 of F_E1 = exactly upstream's
`vreg_l2f_e1`/`vreg_l4f_e1`. **The supplies are correct. There is no mis-wiring.** The
"regulators-3 = C_E1" inference assumed our bank ordering matched the upstream file; it
does not, and the `qcom,pmic-id` is what actually binds.

## Delta 2 (generic panel driver) — **FALSE**

```dts
panel {
        compatible = "samsung,atna60cl08", "samsung,atna33xc20";
        enable-gpios = <0x69 0x12 0x00>;   /* tlmm 18, active high */
        power-supply = <0xfa>;             /* VREG_EDP_3P3, regulator-fixed, gpio tlmm 70 */
};
```

We are already on `panel-samsung-atna33xc20` — with a *more* specific first compatible than
upstream's. The enable GPIO (TLMM 18) and the 3.3 V rail (`VREG_EDP_3P3` on TLMM 70) match
upstream exactly. `CONFIG_DRM_PANEL_SAMSUNG_ATNA33XC20=m` and `dp_aux_backlight` is live, so
the driver is bound. **No delta, and the AMOLED power-sequencing argument does not apply —
we already get it.**

## Delta 3 (eDP HPD) — **TRUE, and it is the only display-wiring delta that exists**

Upstream muxes GPIO119 and points the eDP controller at it:

```dts
&mdss_dp3 { pinctrl-0 = <&edp0_hpd_default>; pinctrl-names = "default"; };
edp0_hpd_default: edp0-hpd-default-state { pins = "gpio119"; function = "edp0_hot"; bias-disable; };
```

Ours cannot: `displayport-controller@af6c000` has **no pinctrl property at all**, and GPIO119
sits inside our `gpio-reserved-ranges` entry `<0x5d 0x35>` = pins 93–145. Our kernel's
pinctrl driver does know the function — `pinctrl-glymur.c:1570`,
`[119] = PINGROUP(119, edp0_hot, edp1_lcd, …)`. **So we have never had hardware eDP HPD.**

## The bigger structural picture (this is the real answer to "how does it differ from ours")

- **Different lineage.** Ours is decompiled from a *vendor* DTB (`model = "Glymur CRD"`,
  `compatible = "qcom,glymur-crd"`) and hand-edited across test55 → test69. Konrad's is
  written from scratch against the in-tree `glymur.dtsi`.
- **Our v7.1 `glymur.dtsi` is a release behind his target.** Building his DTS on our tree
  fails on ten unresolved labels — `gpu`, `gmu`, `lpass_vamacro`, `pcie4_port0_ep`,
  `remoteproc_adsp`, `remoteproc_cdsp`, `remoteproc_soccp`, `swr0`, `swr3` — plus two
  missing audio defines (`VA_CODEC_DMA_TX_0`, `WSA_CODEC_DMA_RX_0`). **None of those nodes
  exist in our `glymur.dtsi` at all.** Our working ADSP, audio and SoCCP battery come from
  the vendor-derived nodes in our own DTB, not from `glymur.dtsi`.
- **TLMM reservations are night and day.** Upstream reserves 12 pins total
  (`<4 4>, <10 2>, <44 4>, <65 1>, <90 1>`). Ours reserves hundreds across 15 ranges. That
  is the root of the whole "deferred is better than broken" test66 saga — and of the missing
  eDP HPD.

⇒ **Adopting Konrad's DTS wholesale is not a one-step move on this tree.** It needs either a
`glymur.dtsi` update from v7.2 (which risks clock-index mismatches against our v7.1 drivers)
or a port of his board wiring onto our older SoC dtsi. Neither is a single variable.

## ✅ What was built instead: test70 = test69 + eDP HPD

The one genuine display delta, as a single variable, built from the **installed** test69 DTB
so it derives from what actually boots:

1. `gpio-reserved-ranges`: `<0x5d 0x35>` → `<0x5d 0x1a 0x78 0x1a>`, freeing **only** pin 119
   (93–118 and 120–145 stay reserved).
2. new `edp0-hpd-default-state` in tlmm — `gpio119`, `function = "edp0_hot"`, `bias-disable`.
3. `displayport-controller@af6c000` gains `pinctrl-names`/`pinctrl-0`.

Verified by decompiling both DTBs and diffing: **those three changes and nothing else.**
Installed at `/boot/glymur/glymur-a16-test70.dtb`, GRUB entry `fedora-glymur-test70`
(backup `/boot/grub/grub.cfg.bak-pre-test70`, default still test69, test69 untouched).

⚠️ Trap check: test66 taught us that freeing a reserved pin without its dependent chain
makes things worse. Pin 119's only consumer is the eDP controller, which is already enabled,
so there is no chain to free — unlike 94/246, which pulled in `wcn7850-pmu` and `1c00000.pci`.
